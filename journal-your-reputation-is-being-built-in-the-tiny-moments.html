Your reputation is being built in the tiny moments

Vanessa BelleauFounder, executive coach & speaker
Share

Most people think reputation is built in the big career moments: the promotion interview, the senior presentation, the huge project or the high-stakes meeting.

Those moments matter.

Yet usually, by the time you get to the big moment, people already have a view of you and that view has often been shaped by hundreds of tiny interactions you barely noticed.

Your professional reputation is being built through small signals.

> You said you would send something by Thursday. Did you?

> You did not understand the brief. Did you ask?

> You could see that a deadline was slipping. Did you flag it?

> Someone gave you feedback you did not love. How did you respond?

> A senior leader joined your meeting. Had you prepared?

> A new colleague arrived. Did you introduce yourself?

> A problem emerged. Did you simply bring the problem or did you start thinking about possible solutions?

» » These are tiny moments that give out big signals.

Over time, people start building a picture of what it is like to work with you:

  • Can they rely on you?
  • Do you think ahead?
  • Do you ask useful questions?
  • Are you open to feedback?
  • Do you communicate clearly when something changes?
  • Do you understand context? Do you make things easier or harder?

And that picture becomes your professional reputation.

BTW, none of this means you need to become obsessed with what everybody thinks of you. Please do not. Yet, it does mean becoming curious about the gap between your intention and your impact.

You might intend to come across as relaxed, but someone else experiences you as underprepared. You might think you are being passionate, while someone else experiences you as not listening. You might think staying quiet shows respect for seniority, while someone else assumes you have nothing to contribute.

The aim is not to police your personality. It is to become more self-aware.

A useful question to ask is:

‘Is the impact I am having aligned with the impact I intended?’

Then pick one signal you want to strengthen. Maybe you want to be known as prepared, reliable, curious, clear, proactive or calm under pressure. Once you choose the signal, ask yourself what behaviour would actually prove it.

Your reputation is rarely created by one dramatic moment.

It is built through consistency.

People remember patterns. How you respond. How you communicate. Whether you follow through. Whether you make things easier or harder.

That means your reputation is being shaped every day, which is actually good news.

Because small behaviours are changeable.

Final thought

Choose one word you want people to associate with you professionally.

Reliable. Curious. Clear. Strategic. Calm. Proactive.

Then identify one behaviour you can repeat this week that proves it.
